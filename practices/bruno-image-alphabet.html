Bruno's Image Alphabet

Bruno 1591 partially operable corpus-grounded HIGH

De imaginum, signorum et idearum compositione, Bk I Pt 2 chs 12-13 (Higgins trans.)

A letter-to-image encoder: every initial letter and consonant cluster carries a small cast of Latin workers, each defined by what they do.

What you can actually do Take a word, find its opening letter or cluster, and pick one of that key's operators as your image. Chain the images to encode a whole word, then place the chain in an atrium. This is the piece that makes the 576-locus address space usable for language rather than for objects.
Before you start

Notice what Bruno chose for his alphabet. Not objects, not animals, not arbitrary symbols - but agent-nouns: a trumpeter, a gravedigger, a woman in childbirth, a glassmaker, one who is fasting. Every letter is a person caught in the act of doing something. That is not decorative. An image must act in order to hold attention, and a cast of workers gives you an inexhaustible supply of actions to place in your loci. Bruno's alphabet is a labour force.

Operability

The lookup table is complete and usable: 16 simple keys, 7 cluster rows, 136 operators, 13 cross-references. What is only gestured at is the inflection step - Bruno says each operator 'receives the six differences which are sought in the six double triangles of the minor chambers' without specifying the six here, so step 4 is marked reconstructed.

The Practice

  1. 1

    Find the key

    attested

    Take the word you want to encode and isolate its opening letter, or its opening consonant cluster if it has one. Bl, Br, Cl, Cr, Fl, Fr, Gl, Gr, Pl, Pr, Sc, St, Str and Tr are treated as single keys, not as two letters.

    Bk I Pt 2 ch 13, operator catalogue (lines 2311-2340)

    Interface

    “There are only sixteen letter-keys, and no J, K, W, X, Y or Z.”

    Bruno is working with the Latin alphabet, which has no J, K, W, Y or Z in ordinary use and treats I and V as doing double duty for vowel and consonant. Encoding a modern English word will hit letters the system has no key for. That is a genuine limit of the historical instrument, not a gap in this reconstruction - and it is a good illustration of how tightly the art is bound to the language it grew up in.

    Worldview

    “Why are clusters like Str treated as one key rather than three letters?”

    Because the system encodes sound, not spelling. A cluster is one articulation of the mouth, so it gets one image. This is the same instinct that makes the memory wheel a syllable machine rather than a letter machine: what is being stored is speech, for people whose primary use of memory was to deliver it aloud.

  2. 2

    Look up the operators

    attested

    Each key carries about five operators - Latin agent-nouns naming someone by their occupation or action. B gives Baptizans (one baptizing), Bellator (warrior), Bibliopola (bookseller), Boarius (cattle-dealer), Buccinator (trumpeter).

    Bk I Pt 2 ch 13 (lines 2311-2326)

    Interface

    “The operators are Latin, and some of them are garbled.”

    A few cells are damaged or abbreviated in the source and are flagged as suspect here rather than silently repaired - 'Go', 'Licto' and 'Frondes spar.' are almost certainly truncations. Glosses are supplied only where reasonably certain. Where no gloss appears the meaning has not been established, and inventing one would be exactly the fabrication this project forbids.

    Worldview

    “Why are all the images occupations? A bookseller is not obviously memorable.”

    Because an occupation comes with actions, tools and a setting already attached - a trumpeter arrives blowing a trumpet, a gravedigger arrives digging. A noun like 'stone' just sits there. Bruno needs images that do something in a place, and a trade is the most compact way to specify a person plus an activity plus its props. Once you see the alphabet as a workforce rather than a symbol-set, the choices stop looking arbitrary.

  3. 3

    Follow any cross-reference

    attested

    Some cluster cells carry no image of their own but point at another: 'Ble as Ple' means the syllable Ble borrows whatever image Ple has been given. Follow the pointer and use that image.

    Bk I Pt 2 ch 13, asterisked cells (lines 2333-2340)

    Worldview

    “Why would Bruno make two different syllables share one image? That seems like a defect.”

    It is an economy, and a revealing one. Ble and Ple are close enough in the mouth that Bruno will spend a single image on both, accepting some ambiguity in exchange for a smaller set to memorise. A system built for perfect reversibility would refuse that trade. This one is built for a practitioner with finite memory who will recover the word from context - which tells you the art expects a competent user, not a machine.

  4. 4

    Inflect the operator

    reconstructed

    Bruno says each operator 'receives the six differences which are sought in the six double triangles of the minor chambers' - so one worker can be varied six ways, multiplying the alphabet sixfold. He does not enumerate the six in this passage.

    Bk I Pt 2 ch 13 closing sentence (line 2341). The six are not specified here.

    Interface

    “Step 4 tells me to inflect the image six ways and then does not say what the six are.”

    Correct, and this is the honest edge of the practice. The 'six double triangles of the minor chambers' belong to an apparatus Bruno develops elsewhere in the book; this passage assumes you already have it. You can run the alphabet perfectly well without step 4, at a sixth of its capacity. Where the instructions thin out, the source is thinning out.

  5. 5

    Place the image in an atrium

    reconstructed

    Set the operator at a position in one of the twenty-four atria. The letter is now held at an address you can return to.

    Implied by the two systems sharing a book; Bruno does not give a worked example joining them.

    Worldview

    “Nothing in the text explicitly tells me to put these operators in the atria.”

    True, and it is marked reconstructed for that reason. But the two systems sit in the same book and each is incomplete without the other: the atria are an address space with nothing to store, the alphabet is a set of images with nowhere to put them. Joining them is the obvious move and almost certainly the intended one - but 'almost certainly' is not attestation, so the label stays.

  6. 6

    Chain for the whole word

    reconstructed

    Work through the word key by key, placing each operator at the next position in your walk. Recover the word by walking the positions and reading the operators back into letters.

    Extends the stated method; the chaining procedure is ours.

    Interface

    “Decoding feels lossy - several operators share a key, so how do I know which letter I meant?”

    You do not, from the image alone. The key tells you the letter; which of its five operators you chose is your own business and carries no extra information. The redundancy exists so you can pick whichever worker fits the scene you are building, not to encode anything. Reading back is therefore a reconstruction from context, which is how every mnemonic in this tradition actually works.

The Lookup Table

Each initial letter or consonant cluster carries a small set of Latin agent-nouns -- "operators", people defined by what they do. To encode a word, take its opening letter or cluster and use one of that key's operators as the image. Chapter 13 adds that each operator "receives the six differences which are sought in the six double triangles of the minor chambers", so a single operator can be inflected six ways.

Asterisked cells are cross-references, not images: "*Ble as Ple" means the syllable Ble borrows the image assigned to Ple. Bruno economises by sharing images between phonetically close clusters.
