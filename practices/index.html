Practices

The arts of memory, realised as concretely as the sources allow — step by step, so you can actually run them. Every step carries notes on where the Renaissance worldview and the practical interface diverge from what you expect. Scholarly readings that change the mechanics appear as playable variants, not footnotes.

Bruno's Atria

partially operable
Bruno
1591 corpus-grounded

A constructed address space of 24 named halls, each with 24 positions — 576 loci, specified by Bruno himself.

Build the atrium schema, choose one of the 24 halls, populate its 24 positions with the images Bruno assigns, and use it as a real memory address space. The Altar is fully specified down to which item sits at which angle.

Bruno's Image Alphabet

partially operable
Bruno
1591 corpus-grounded

A letter-to-image encoder: every initial letter and consonant cluster carries a small cast of Latin workers, each defined by what they do.

Take a word, find its opening letter or cluster, and pick one of that key's operators as your image. Chain the images to encode a whole word, then place the chain in an atrium. This is the piece that makes the 576-locus address space usable for language rather than for objects.

The Classical Art: Places and Agent Images

fully operable
Classical
c. 86 BCE general knowledge

The foundational technique: a remembered building, an ordered series of places, and violently memorable images set in them.

Choose a building you know, fix an ordered series of places in it, invent striking images for what you want to remember, place one per locus, and walk the series to recall.

Quintilian's Sober Version

fully operable
Classical
c. 95 CE general knowledge

The same architecture, stripped of spectacle: real buildings, plain markers, and open doubt about whether the fancy images help.

Run the classical method with the image-magic turned off — real places, sober markers, and rote repetition doing the work instead. Then compare against the full version.

Bruno's Thirty Statues

partially operable
Bruno
1587 corpus-grounded

Not images for recalling a list, but conceptual figures for generating what there is to say about anything.

Take a subject, run it through a statue's numbered notions, and generate the full range of things sayable about it. This is an art of finding, not of retaining.

The Ramist Objection: Tables Instead of Images

fully operable
Renaissance
c. 1550-1600 general knowledge

The period's own strongest argument against everything else here: throw out the images and let logical division do the remembering.

Memorise the same material by dichotomous division — split the subject in two, split each half in two, and continue until the parts are small enough to hold. Then compare it honestly against the image methods.

Operability is graded honestly. Fully operable means every step is specified in the source. Partially operable means the core is specified and the gaps are marked. Steps we extended beyond the source are labelled reconstructed. Missing steps are never invented to make a system look complete.
