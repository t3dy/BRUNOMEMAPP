The Classical Art: Places and Agent Images

Classical c. 86 BCE fully operable general knowledge MEDIUM

Rhetorica ad Herennium III.16-24

The foundational technique: a remembered building, an ordered series of places, and violently memorable images set in them.

What you can actually do Choose a building you know, fix an ordered series of places in it, invent striking images for what you want to remember, place one per locus, and walk the series to recall.
Before you start

This art was invented for people who had to deliver long speeches from memory, in a world with little paper, no index, and no way to look anything up mid-sentence. Memory was not a private convenience; it was a professional instrument and, later, a moral virtue. That is the pressure that makes the rest of it make sense.

Operability

Every step is specified in the source. This is the baseline against which Bruno's departures become legible — learn it first.

The Practice

  1. 1

    Choose a building you know well

    attested

    Pick a real place you can walk in imagination without hesitating — a house you lived in, a church, a route you have taken hundreds of times. It must be quiet, well-lit, and not crowded with similar-looking rooms.

    Ad Herennium III.19 (from general knowledge; text not in corpus)

    Interface

    “I do not have a building I know that well.”

    This is the single biggest practical gap between the ancient user and you. The art assumes a life spent in a small number of buildings entered thousands of times. Modern life is high-turnover and increasingly screen-mediated, and many people genuinely lack a deeply known interior. Use the place you have spent the most hours in even if it is dull — a childhood home, a workplace, a supermarket route. Dullness is fine; familiarity is not optional.

  2. 2

    Fix an ordered series of places

    attested

    Walk a fixed route and select distinct positions along it — a doorway, a corner, a window, a hearth. Keep them moderately spaced and visually unlike one another. The order must never vary.

    Ad Herennium III.19

    Worldview

    “Why must the order never vary? I would rather jump straight to item seven.”

    You cannot. There is no random access here: memory is a path you walk, not an index you query. This is the deepest habit-mismatch in the whole art, because search has trained you to expect direct retrieval. The ordered walk is not a limitation the ancients failed to overcome — it is the data structure. The every-fifth-place marker in the next step is their answer to exactly your complaint.

  3. 3

    Mark every fifth place

    attested

    Put a distinguishing mark at every fifth locus — a golden hand at the fifth, an acquaintance named Decimus at the tenth. This lets you count into the series without walking it from the start.

    Ad Herennium III.19

    Interface

    “Why every fifth place specifically?”

    Because you count on one hand. The five-marker lets you jump into the middle of a long series without walking it from the beginning — it is the ancient answer to the random-access complaint, and it is calibrated to the body rather than to base ten. The tenth gets a second marker because that is two hands.

  4. 4

    Make agent images

    attested

    Invent images that act rather than sit. Make them exceptionally beautiful or exceptionally ugly; give them motion; stain them with blood or mud; smear them with red paint; make them comic or obscene. Ordinary things slip away; the extraordinary stays.

    Ad Herennium III.22

    Interface

    “Can I just use images someone else made?”

    Poorly. Images work through your associations, and a borrowed image carries someone else's. Every serious source insists you make your own, which is also why an app cannot do the interesting part for you. What a tool can do is hold the architecture and the order; the images have to be yours.

    Worldview

    “Bloodied, obscene, disfigured images feel gratuitous, even nasty.”

    They are a technical requirement, not a taste. The underlying psychology holds that the mind retains what strikes it and lets the ordinary slide off; so emotional shock is being used as a fixative. The theory long predates any distinction between a memory technique and an affective manipulation — nobody involved thought there was a line to cross. If the images feel excessive to you, that reaction is the historical gap doing its work, and it is worth noticing rather than suppressing.

  5. 5

    Place one image per locus

    attested

    Set each image in its place, interacting with the architecture — leaning on the doorframe, spilling into the corner. One image per locus; do not crowd them.

    Ad Herennium III.20

    Interface

    “Why only one image per place? I have more to remember than I have doorways.”

    Two images in one locus interfere and you lose both — the failure is reliable enough that every source warns about it. The answer is more places, not denser ones, which is why practitioners invested years in extending their architecture. If you are short of loci you have found the real constraint of the art, and it is the constraint Bruno's 576-address system is built to solve.

  6. 6

    Walk to recall

    attested

    Recall by walking the route in order and reading off what you find. To recall backwards, walk it in reverse. The places persist; the images are wiped and replaced for the next use.

    Ad Herennium III.21

    Worldview

    “Wiping the images to reuse the places seems wasteful — why not keep them?”

    Because places are expensive and images are cheap. Building a reliable ordered series takes weeks; inventing an image takes seconds. The architecture is the durable asset and the content is disposable, which is precisely the opposite of how a modern user thinks about storage. Bruno's fixed twenty-four atria push this logic to its conclusion.
