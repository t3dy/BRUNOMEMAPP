The Ramist Objection: Tables Instead of Images

Renaissance c. 1550-1600 fully operable general knowledge MEDIUM

Petrus Ramus and the Ramist tradition

The period's own strongest argument against everything else here: throw out the images and let logical division do the remembering.

What you can actually do Memorise the same material by dichotomous division — split the subject in two, split each half in two, and continue until the parts are small enough to hold. Then compare it honestly against the image methods.
Before you start

By the later sixteenth century a serious movement held that the whole apparatus of places and grotesque images was superstitious clutter, and that properly ordered knowledge remembers itself. This is why the image tradition withers in Protestant Europe while Bruno is still elaborating it — and it is a large part of why Bruno looked suspect to his contemporaries. Run it, and you are running the objection to everything else on this list.

Operability

The method is simple and completely specifiable. Included because omitting it would present the art of memory as uncontested in its own time, which it was not.

The Practice

  1. 1

    State the whole subject

    attested

    Write the subject as a single heading at the left.

    General knowledge; Ramist texts not in corpus.

    Worldview

    “Writing the subject as a single heading assumes it has clean edges.”

    It does assume that, and the assumption is the method's central bet: that knowledge comes in bounded subjects which can be exhaustively analysed. The image tradition makes no such bet — a memory palace will happily hold unrelated junk in an arbitrary order. Ramism trades that tolerance for structure, and the trade only pays if the world really is that tidy.

  2. 2

    Divide in two

    attested

    Split it into exactly two parts that between them exhaust it and do not overlap.

    General knowledge.

    Worldview

    “Real subjects do not divide neatly in two.”

    That objection was made at the time and it is a good one. The Ramists held that if a subject resists clean bifurcation you have not yet understood it — the method is a claim about the orderliness of knowledge itself, not merely a note-taking format. Whether the world obliges is precisely what is in dispute, and the strain you feel forcing a division is the argument happening in your hands.

  3. 3

    Repeat to the leaves

    attested

    Divide each part in two again, and continue until each remaining part is small enough to hold in one thought. Draw the result as a bracketed table opening left to right.

    General knowledge.

    Interface

    “Why draw it as brackets opening left to right rather than as a list?”

    Because the shape is the argument: everything at one depth is a genuine division of the thing to its left, and the eye can verify that at a glance in a way a nested list hides. Printed Ramist tables were often single large fold-out sheets for exactly this reason — the whole structure had to be visible at once. Modern outliners collapse branches and lose that property.

  4. 4

    Recall by descending the table

    attested

    To retrieve anything, walk down the branches. The structure of the subject is the structure of the memory; no images are used at any point.

    General knowledge.

    Interface

    “This just feels like an ordinary outline.”

    It is — that is the point, and its victory is why it feels unremarkable. Bracketed tables, nested bullets and collapsible outlines all descend from this. The reason the classical art now looks exotic and the Ramist table looks like common sense is that one of them won.

    Worldview

    “Why did anyone think the image method was dangerous rather than just inefficient?”

    Because vivid interior images of gods, planets and violent figures looked to reforming eyes like idolatry conducted inside the skull, and because the more ambitious claims for the art shaded into magic. The Ramist preference for bare logical structure is partly a technical judgement and partly a religious one. Bruno, who pushed the images further than anyone, was burned in 1600 — not for his mnemonics, but the suspicion was of a piece.
