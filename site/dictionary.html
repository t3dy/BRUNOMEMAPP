Dictionary

Bruno’s key vocabulary — memoria, phantasia, simulacrum, signaculum, umbra, vinculum, and more — with his own usage and scholarly interpretation held apart.

appetitus

Latin MEDIUM

Appetite, desire; the faculty of the soul that inclines toward or away from things.

Bruno’s usage: Bruno's 'higher art' explicitly claims to order the operations of the soul including appetition, not just intellect.

Scholarly interpretation: Clucas: central to the ethical dimension of the memory art — training desire/disposition is as much the point as training cognition.

imaginatio

Latin HIGH

Imagination; the faculty that generates and holds mnemonic/magical images.

Bruno’s usage: Works alongside intellect: intellect grasps a universal relation, imagination embodies it in a memorable, psychologically operative image.

Scholarly interpretation: Couliano treats it as the operative medium of psychological binding (vincula); Clucas treats it as intellect's necessary embodying complement.

intentio

Latin MEDIUM

Intention, in the medieval-scholastic technical sense of a mental content directed at an object; the organizing unit of Bruno's 'Thirty Intentions of the Shadows.'

Bruno’s usage: Structures the descent-from-unity scheme in De umbris idearum's Thirty Intentions.

Scholarly interpretation: Clucas: the Thirty Intentions present a Neoplatonic theory of knowledge derived from Plotinus via Ficino.

memoria

Latin HIGH

Memory, but for Bruno (following Plotinus) an active power of soul rather than passive storage.

Bruno’s usage: The faculty trained and reorganized by the mnemonic art; not a container for impressions but an activity the soul performs.

Scholarly interpretation: Clucas traces this directly to Plotinus's Ennead IV.6 rejection of the wax-tablet model.

phantasia

Latin/Greek MEDIUM

Imagination in its more technical, philosophical-psychological sense (vs. imaginatio's broader usage); the intermediary faculty between sensation and intellect.

Bruno’s usage: Not merely a defective intermediary (per Wang) — capable of producing iconic forms that function as vehicles of ascent toward knowledge.

Scholarly interpretation: Wang: positive cognitive faculty. Couliano: medium of desire/attention/binding.

sigillum

Latin MEDIUM

Seal. A structured mental/magical operation, as in the Thirty Seals and the Seal of Seals.

Bruno’s usage: Names both individual techniques (the Thirty Seals) and the master unifying technique (Sigillus Sigillorum, the Seal of Seals).

Scholarly interpretation: Distinct from but related to signaculum; grounds the proposed 'Seal Lab' interactive mode.

signaculum

Latin MEDIUM

A little sign or seal; a marked/sealed image with a more explicitly operative (magical) function than a general simulacrum.

Bruno’s usage: Appears alongside simulacrum in Clucas's paired title 'Simulacra et Signacula'; central to the Seal texts (Explicatio triginta sigillorum, Sigillus sigillorum).

Scholarly interpretation: Clucas pairs it with simulacrum as two aspects (image and seal) of the same operative image-theory.

simulacrum

Latin HIGH

A likeness or image; for Bruno, a constructed image that participates in an ordered system of relations rather than merely resembling something.

Bruno’s usage: Used for mnemonic-magical images throughout De umbris and De imaginum.

Scholarly interpretation: Clucas: instrument for ordering the soul's operations. Mertens: instrument regulating cognitive/spiritual forces, richer than 'occult symbol.'

species

Latin LOW

A form or likeness transmitted from an object to a perceiving/knowing subject; part of the scholastic-derived causal vocabulary Bruno adapts.

Bruno’s usage: Used in the theory of how images and objects transmit their form/influence, in De magia and related texts.

Scholarly interpretation: Part of the technical apparatus scholars use to reconstruct HOW Bruno thought images causally operated, alongside spiritus and vinculum.

spiritus

Latin MEDIUM

Spirit; the mediating substance/medium through which causal influence (natural or magical) is thought to travel between bodies, souls, and the cosmos.

Bruno’s usage: Part of the causal vocabulary in De magia explaining how images/bonds actually operate.

Scholarly interpretation: Mertens: mnemonic technique regulates and protects the spirits involved in cognition, connecting memory to protection from demonic corruption.

umbra

Latin MEDIUM

Shadow. Bruno's term for mnemonic images (umbrae idearum, 'shadows of ideas').

Bruno’s usage: Titles his foundational treatise De umbris idearum. Ambiguous between 'shadows cast BY ideas' (derogatory copy) and 'the shadow-like way ideas appear in the sensible world' (dynamic metaphysical relation).

Scholarly interpretation: Clucas reads shadows as ordered expressions of unity descending into multiplicity, per the Plotinian One-to-many structure; the mnemonic operation traces shadows back toward their source.

vinculum

Latin HIGH

Bond, tie. The relationship an image/operator establishes between imagination, desire, and a target.

Bruno’s usage: Central subject of the treatise De vinculis in genere; the mechanism by which images/attention/desire link magician to target.

Scholarly interpretation: Couliano's central concept: magical efficacy as psychological binding via phantasia and desire, not (necessarily) supernatural force transmission.
