Bibliography

Primary and secondary sources cited throughout this project.

Frances A. Yates (1964)

PRIMARY monograph

Giordano Bruno and the Hermetic Tradition. Foundational but partially superseded reconstruction of the memory wheel.

Frances A. Yates (1966)

PRIMARY monograph

The Art of Memory. Essential context for the Renaissance memory tradition Bruno inherits.

Ioan P. Couliano (1987)

PRIMARY monograph

Eros and Magic in the Renaissance. Psychological/vinculum reading of Renaissance magical images.

Rita Sturlese (1991)

PRIMARY critical_edition

Critical edition of De umbris idearum. Corrects Yates's planetary placement in the wheel reconstruction.

Francesco Torchia (1997)

PRIMARY article

Analysis extending Sturlese's wheel correction. Confirms and extends the Sturlese correction.

Hilary Gatti (1999)

CONTEXTUAL monograph

Giordano Bruno and Renaissance Science. Broader philosophical/scientific context.

Stephen Clucas (1999)

PRIMARY article

Amorem, artem, magiam, mathesim: Brunian Images and the Domestication of the Soul. Key statement of the logic+ethics+magic synthetic reading.

Alessandro Farinella and Carole Preston (2002)

DIRECT article

Article on the memory wheel and Bruno's philosophical synthesis. Qualitative-mathematics reading of the wheel's geometry.

Hilary Gatti (ed.) (2002)

PRIMARY edited_volume

Giordano Bruno: Philosopher of the Renaissance. Contains Clucas's 'Simulacra et Signacula: Memory, Magic and Metaphysics in Brunian Mnemonics.'

Manuel Mertens (2018)

PRIMARY monograph

Magic and Memory in Giordano Bruno: The Art of a Heroic Spirit. Integrates memory and magic via spirit-regulation and heroic transformation.

Aleksandar Ostojić (2020)

DIRECT article

Article on the historiography of Bruno's ars memoriae. Names the magic/science historiographical binary problem.

Julián Barenstein (2022)

PRIMARY article

Memoria regulata: hacia una interpretación no esotérica del De umbris idearum. De-esotericizing counterweight position.

Zheng Wang (2022)

PRIMARY article

The Role of Iconic Practice in Bruno's Gnoseology. Image as instrument of knowing, not passive storage.
